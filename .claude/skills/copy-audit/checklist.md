# BG persuasion checklist (15 principles + guardrails)

Source: the 15-principle persuasion post Andy pasted on 26 Oct 2026 ("create a checklist and tell it to audit its output against it"). Adapted to BusinessGrowR: we sell growth (ads, funnels, sales teams, Whop) to **trading Discord owners and trading educators**, positioned anti-guru, with a guarantee offer.

Each item is a yes/no question. Mark every item **PASS**, **GAP** or **N/A** (with the reason it doesn't apply to this format). A GAP needs a quoted line and a concrete rewrite.

---

## Part A. Guardrails (run first; any FAIL blocks delivery)

These are BG rules. Where a principle below seems to say otherwise, these win.

| # | Check |
|---|---|
| G1 | Every claim, number and story is a `USE` or `SOURCED` line from the messaging bank (`/mnt/project-files/content/messaging-bank/andy-positions-and-lines-2026-10-04.md`), or Andy said it in this task. Nothing `CHECK` or `HOLD`. |
| G2 | Zero hits against bank section 8 ("Lines NOT to use") and the client-name rules (`creative-rules-quick` memory). "$20M+ in revenue for trading education businesses" and "$6.4M+ in sales on our own Whop" are allowed. |
| G3 | No trading-performance claims about any client's product (win rates, payouts, "prints"). |
| G4 | Associations (principle 9) never imply someone is a client, endorses us, or used our method. The post's "it doesn't matter if it's true" does NOT apply to us: the association must be a true fact about the anchor. |
| G5 | Urgency and scarcity are real and their reason is stated (principle 4). No fake deadlines, fake "Re:" subjects or bait. |
| G6 | Surface rules: client X posts and IG captions never mention or tag Whop; IG links untagged; X posts close on one plain-advice sentence, never an aphorism. |
| G7 | Paid ads: no line that asserts a negative personal attribute of the viewer ("Are you broke?", "Your Discord is dying"). Talk about the business or the situation, not the person. | G8 | Andy's voice: short sentences, no em dashes, hedges kept where the bank has them ("generally", "in our experience"), never hardened to "most" or "every". |

## Part B. The 15 principles

**1. Identity mirroring.** Does the copy name and mirror an identity the avatar already holds, so the ask feels consistent with who they think they are?
BG identities: the owner who treats the Discord like a business (journals trades, not the business: bank section 4 item 37), the educator who teaches, not a signals seller (F9), the builder who wants a real company, not a hobby chat. Mirror example: "Serious educators track their trades. The ones who scale track their funnel the same way."

**2. Perception is reality.** Does the copy work with the market's existing perceptions instead of arguing against them?
BG market perceptions: agencies and gurus overpromise; "just post more content" advice; Fiverr can build a funnel. Position with them, anti-guru: we run our own Whop ($6.4M+), we hate fluff too, and the hard part is the data, not the build (bank section 2 item 5, section 4 item 19). Never argue "not all agencies are scams".

**3. Emotion first, logical narrative second.** Is there one clear core emotion (fear of staying stuck, FOMO as others scale, pride) AND a socially acceptable logical line the owner can repeat to justify acting ("I'd treat any other business this way", "the data says views don't pay, leads do")?

**4. Justification looping.** Is every claim justified, including the offer itself? Ask: why is the offer this generous (why do we guarantee it)? Why now (state the real reason)? Why limited (real capacity reason)? Big claims carry proof right next to them.

**5. Delivery matches the message.** (Spoken formats: ads, VSL, reels, YouTube.) Do pacing, tone and cadence fit the premise? An anti-guru or skeptical-market piece sounds calm, plain and native, not hype. Delivery notes go in the run order or editor notes, never in the teleprompter text.

**6. "Will this work for MY situation?"** Does the copy pre-handle the top situational objections for this angle?
Common ones for our avatar: small community / few followers (section 4 items 24 and 33, safe version), lots of views but few members, a niche other than ICT (options, futures, crypto), sells signals or alerts, no time to make content, tried an agency before. List the ones this angle raises; mark any that are unaddressed.

**7. Autonomy, competence, relatedness.** Does the copy speak to the threatened need? Competence is the usual one for us: "you already know how to trade and teach, you're missing the system behind the CTA" (section 4 item 31). Autonomy: own your audience off-platform (email, Discord) instead of depending on the algorithm. Relatedness: what the big Discords do.

**8. Dimensionalization.** Is at least one abstract line made concrete enough to see, hear or feel? Swap "low retention" for "they take three signals, lose money, and they're gone by the end of the month" (belief 2). Check the problem line and the change-state line first.

**9. Association.** Is the mechanism tied to an anchor the market idolizes, without claiming endorsement (G4)?
BG anchors that are true and sourced: TJR's free YouTube bootcamp (S7), how the big Discords spend more time on acquisition than product (belief 4), treating trading like a business. Never imply an anchor is a client.

**10. Pattern interrupt hook.** Does the first line break "sameness" with one of: weird culprit / hidden mechanism, statistical impossibility (sourced numbers only), attacking an accepted rule ("Posting more content is why your Discord isn't growing"), herd-follower ("what the big Discords do that small ones don't"), unspoken truth, or an external catalyst (a real platform change, e.g. DM restrictions, a Whop or Meta update)?

**11. Loss over gain.** Is at least one hook or key line framed as a loss (members churning, money left on the table, views that never become buyers)? If every hook is gain-framed, write a flipped version.

**12. Unknown mapped to known.** When a new idea or the mechanism appears, is it explained with a metaphor the market already understands (a trading metaphor works best: "your funnel has no stop loss", "you're trading without a journal")? Check it doesn't clash with a common association (13b).

**13. Low cognitive load.** (a) ONE core idea, ONE problem, ONE mechanism, ONE CTA. Count them; more than one of any is a GAP. (b) No gaps in the argument: every "X is dead, so buy Y" has the because-bridge in between. No metaphor that fights a common association.

**14. Belief shift without backfire.** Does the copy validate the old belief first ("you're right that posting more didn't work"), then open a narrow wedge (a mechanism or an outside authority), and, for negative self-beliefs, move the blame to a common enemy (generic growth advice, not the owner)? Never name a real company or person as the enemy.

**15. Slippery slide.** Read it top to bottom: does each block (hook, problem, mechanism, proof, offer, CTA) resolve the one before? Mark any jump where a new premise appears with no bridge.
