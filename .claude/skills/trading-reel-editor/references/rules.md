# Editing rules (from the Advanced Trading Content course)

These are the course's rules that an editor can apply or check. Where the course and the reel study
disagree, the note says which to follow. Numbers in [brackets] are course lessons.

## Script shape (check before editing)
- Structure: hook, sub-hook, retain, re-hook, reward, re-hook, CTA. [3]
- The hook makes a spoken promise of a payoff ("in the next 60 seconds I'll show you...") and the rest
  of the video keeps it. Name the creator's unique mechanism in the hook when there is one. [5, 7, 15]
- Right after the hook, about 3 s of proof that backs the promise (the sub-hook): a P&L, a result, a
  number. Every teaching reel should have one. [7]
- Hook emotions that work: easy, safe, new, big. Cut hedges ("I think", "maybe"). [6, 15]
- Re-hook right after the first small payoff (about 15-20 s into a 60 s reel) and again right before
  the CTA ("one more thing"). A re-hook can be a 0.3-0.5 s pattern break or a 5 s new promise. [3, 8]
- Show the payoff, don't only say it: let the candles play out to the target. [3, 9]
- Most reels should speak to traders who already know the problem (about 80%), not beginners. No
  beginner explainers, no off-strategy topics, no silent sped-up live trading with P&L. [4, 13]

## Cutting
- Cut every pause and filler ("um", "okay so now moving on to..."), hardest in the hook. [5, 6, 12]
- Do not speed the whole video up. Trim instead; slow down only for the key point. [12]
- Speak as fast as stays clear to nearly everyone; slower for a strong accent. [11]

## Visuals
- First drawing on the chart within 2 s (ideally at once), then something changes at least every
  2 s; never more than about 5 s without a cut, zoom, scroll or new drawing. [1, 9]
- Drawings are live and land the moment the creator says the thing. Flag a drawing that arrives
  seconds late. [9]
- Mix shots: chart zoom, chart scroll, switch between camera and chart. [9, 12]
- Stack the webcam and chart vertically, browser bars cropped off so only the chart shows. [1]
- Clean background and even light on the face. [10, 15]
- Don't use edits to hide weak visuals: fix the recording instead. [9]

## Text on screen
- Hook title on screen from the first frame, white on a black box; captions only after it leaves. [10]
- Captions: plain, one word at a time, on the line between camera and chart. No animated, flashy
  caption packs. [2, 9, 10, 15]
  - Reel study: matches the teaching format. Face (ad) reels in the study used bold 1-3 word caps
    with colour on numbers and keywords; use that look only for the face format.
- Keyword CTA burned on screen, large, during the CTA. [16]

## Never add
- Sound effects, whooshes, stock footage, AI voice-over, flashy transitions, or music under a
  teaching voice. [2, 9] (The reel study agrees: every top teaching reel was voice only.)

## CTA
- At the end, never at the start (exception: a "comment 1 or 2" engagement reel asks early). [16, 18]
- Order: proof, benefit, what they get, then "comment WORD and I'll DM it to you for free". The
  freebie is a guide/checklist, not a trial. [16]
- Reel study note: CTA reels didn't get more views than no-CTA reels. Leave the CTA off joke/POV reels.

## Cadence (for planning a batch)
- At least one short a day; three a day is the recommendation. [2]
- About 80% problem-aware topics, 10% solution-aware, 10% offer-aware; none aimed at total beginners. [13]
